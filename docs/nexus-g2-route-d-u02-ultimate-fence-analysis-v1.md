## 3. Focused external deadline facts and limits

N03 documents STOP/DELETE, minimum 30 seconds and maximum 120 days, and up to 30 seconds after the specified cutoff to BEGIN stopping/deleting. Reset/reboot retains terminationTimestamp; stop/suspend clears it. Restart/resume recomputes a relative duration, but an absolute time is reused and rerun after expiry fails until that time is changed/removed. Updates are possible. The read-only calculated timestamp does not make the configurable schedule immutable. [Compute Engine limited runtime](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/limit-vm-runtime).

N04 describes ordinary scheduled stop/deletion: a non-Spot guest gets up to 120 seconds for shutdown, remaining scripts are forcibly stopped, then mechanical-off and finalization occur. 'Within a few seconds' is not a numerical worst-case bound for external failure or survivor cleanup. [Compute Engine stop semantics](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/suspend-stop-reset-instances-overview).

N05 explicitly disallows enabled graceful shutdown on limited-runtime instances. Therefore do not import an additional PENDING_STOP allowance into this candidate by assuming both modes coexist. Exact compatible configuration still needs evidence. [Graceful shutdown restrictions](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/graceful-shutdown-overview).

INFERENCE: a provider-resident preconfigured action can plausibly survive guest Agent/manager/kernel loss and Controller/network loss, because it resides outside those guest processes. Public documentation does not establish a hard completion bound during provider hypervisor/control-plane/physical-host correlated faults. The nominal intervals above cannot be summed into a universal 150-second completion guarantee. A cutoff cannot be established by logging into an account in this run.

PROPOSAL for future review, not an implemented stack: independently issued absolute cutoff; independently authenticated arm acknowledgment tied to original VM and conserved attempt slot; no runtime actor schedule/start/resume/recreation authority; no auto-healing, instance group, snapshot restoration or boot autostart; no workload-visible infrastructure identity/control channel; default-deny after boot. If provisioning is itself an effect, either a documented create-time schedule must already cover it or a preexisting outer envelope must cover the pre-create phase. A deadline first installed after allocation leaves an unprotected interval. No fact here proves that a selected product covers that interval or every declared fault.
